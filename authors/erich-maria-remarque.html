Erich Maria Remarque

All Quiet on the Western Front (1929)

I'm unsure of how to go about writing about my experience with this book. To start with the topical matters, it is written with clarity and ease of language that I hope to be able to imitate or at least come across more often.

The experiences Paul goes through are one thing in and of themselves, but it's in the way he speaks of others in the same situation that gives some new layer of depth that I'm struggling to really describe. It's raw, passionate at times but not in the sense of compassion or romanticism but on the edge of cynicism, irony, really. It is realistic but not without character and emotion. Not without pain. There is death everywhere especially within those who live to speak — and in this case write — about it.
