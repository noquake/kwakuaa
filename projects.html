Homelab — virtualization, storage, and backup infrastructure

● running|[YYYY] – present|self-directed

Stack: Proxmox VE · TrueNAS SCALE · ZFS · Proxmox Backup Server · Tailscale · MikroTik RouterOS · Docker · Linux

ProblemI wanted enterprise operations experience without an enterprise to practice on: virtualization, network-attached storage, segmented networking, secure remote access, and backups that are actually verified — not just assumed to work.

ApproachI built it in nodes instead of one box. A custom AM4 server runs Proxmox VE as the hypervisor, with TrueNAS SCALE virtualized on top of it through HBA passthrough so the NAS gets direct control of its disks and ZFS can do its job honestly. A second, dedicated machine runs Proxmox Backup Server, because backups that live on the same hardware as the data aren't backups. Remote access goes through a Tailscale mesh with a subnet router instead of open ports, and an L3-capable switch handles segmentation on the wire.

SolutionA multi-node lab that mirrors a small production environment: virtualized workloads, ZFS-backed storage, scheduled and tested backups, and zero ports forwarded to the internet. It also hosts real services with real users (see nyansape below), which keeps me honest — downtime here isn't hypothetical.

nyansape — a self-hosted library system

● running|[YYYY] – present|self-directed

Stack: Koha ILS · MariaDB · Debian VPS · cron · mysqldump · Tailscale · Proxmox Backup Server

ProblemI'm interested in what it takes to run library infrastructure — catalog, circulation, patron records — as managed infrastructure, with Ghanaian institutions as the long-term use case. Reading about integrated library systems wasn't going to teach me their failure modes. Running one would.

ApproachI deployed Koha, the open-source ILS, on a VPS: public catalog on one subdomain, staff interface on another. The part I treated as non-negotiable was the backup path. A nightly cron job dumps the database with --single-transaction for consistency, bundles the instance configuration, and ships it over Tailscale to the Proxmox Backup Server in my homelab — off-provider, on hardware I control, authenticated with a scoped API token rather than root credentials.

SolutionA production library system with an automated, off-site, restorable backup pipeline. The name comes from nyansapɔ, the Akan wisdom-knot. Next step is migrating it off the VPS and onto the homelab entirely.

Pathways — clinical RAG pipeline for a children's hospital

○ completed|Sept 2025 – May 2026|City of Hartford Applied AI Center · with Ki Hwang

Stack: Docker (multi-container) · PostgreSQL + pgvector · Docling · SentenceTransformers · REST APIs · Keycloak · React · VPS deployment

ProblemConnecticut Children's Medical Center keeps its emergency treatment guidance — clinical pathways — across more than 2,000 pages of dense PDFs. In an emergency department, the time it takes a practitioner to find the right page is time that matters.

ApproachMy partner and I built a retrieval-augmented generation pipeline as our senior capstone, working directly with the hospital's CMIO and clinical informatics leadership through the Applied AI Center's inaugural Innovation Sandbox cohort. Docling handles ingestion of the clinical PDFs, embeddings are stored in PostgreSQL with pgvector, and the whole system runs as a multi-container Docker deployment behind REST APIs, with Keycloak for authentication and a React frontend. Consultations with technical teams at Seattle Children's and CDW shaped the hosting, reliability, and maintenance decisions — we were building for the people who would inherit the system, not for a demo.

SolutionA deployed, queryable clinical decision-support prototype that turns a 2,000-page document set into direct answers with sourcing. It won Best Presentation & Poster at the senior capstone showcase. Source: github.com/noquake/Pathways_Prototype.

Research & Writing

Cloud responsibility and data sovereignty in emerging markets

○ completed|Spring 2026|CPSC 395, Trinity College

Method: security research · case study analysis · primary-source interview · IEEE citation

ProblemThe cloud shared responsibility model assumes a stable division of labor between provider and customer. The 2024 Change Healthcare breach and a wave of data-sovereignty decisions — including Ghana's rejection of a US health data deal — suggest that assumption is breaking down along regional lines.

ApproachI argued the model will fragment into regional regimes within a decade, using Change Healthcare as the framing case study and grounding the emerging-markets analysis in a primary-source interview with the founder of a Ghanaian cloud company, plus my earlier work on Ghana's national identity systems.

SolutionA term paper connecting cloud architecture to policy — the kind of systems question I want to keep working on.

ProblemGhana's national identity infrastructure carries real architectural and governance risks, and most proposals for it import assumptions from systems built elsewhere.

ApproachI evaluated Ghana's identity systems against the EU Digital Identity (EUDI) framework to see which of its design lessons transfer and which don't.

SolutionA research paper that became the foundation for my continuing work on infrastructure in Ghana — and a source I've since cited in later research.

Dante en France — senior honors thesis

○ completed|Fall 2025 – May 2026|advised by Walid Bouchakour

Method: French academic prose · LaTeX · intellectual history

ProblemDante's twentieth-century French reception runs through two very different readers — the philosopher Étienne Gilson and the poet Paul Claudel — and the humanist thread connecting them is easy to miss if you only read one.

ApproachI wrote the thesis in French, tracing both readings of the Divine Comedy, and typeset it in LaTeX with full French typographic conventions.

SolutionAn honors thesis in Francophone Studies. It has nothing to do with computer science, which is exactly why it's here: the same discipline that structures a system structures an argument.
